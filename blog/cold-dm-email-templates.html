Cold DM and email templates to reach startup founders

I sent 200+ job applications and almost nothing came back. Then I started messaging founders directly. About 30% replied, and I got 3-4 offers. These are the exact formats I used.

Why this works

  • I sent 200+ job applications. Almost nothing came back.
  • Then I started messaging founders directly on LinkedIn. About 30% replied.
  • Founder interviews skip DSA and system design. They talk about their real project and the problem they are stuck on.
  • Usually it is one technical round, then a round with the founder, then the offer. I got 3-4 offers this way.
Hard truthCold DMs have low odds. Most people will not reply. So send many, keep each one short, and make each one personal.

How to use these

  • Copy a template. Replace everything in [orange brackets]. Delete the brackets.
  • Keep it under 80 words. Founders read on their phone.
  • Always add one project with a link. One real project beats a list of skills.
  • Say how you can help them, not only what you want.
  • Make the first line personal: their funding, launch, post or product.
  • Follow up once after 3-4 days. Then move on.

1. LinkedIn DM to a founder

Use when: a startup just raised money, launched something, or posted about hiring.

LinkedIn DM

Hi [Name],

Congrats on [funding / launch / recent post]. Really liked what [Company] is doing with [their product or problem].

I'm a [your role] with [X] years of experience in [skill 1], [skill 2] and [skill 3]. [Degree, College] (optional).

Recently I built [project name]: [what it does in one line], [result or number].
Link: [GitHub / demo link]

I think I can help [Company] with [one specific thing you can build or fix].

Open to a quick chat? Happy to share my resume.

Thanks,
[Your Name]

2. LinkedIn DM for a referral

Use when: you found an open role and want a referral from someone working there (an employee or the hiring manager).

LinkedIn DM

Hi [Name],

Saw the [Role] opening at [Company] ([Location], Job ID: [ID]). It looks like a great fit for my profile.

I'm a [your role] with [X] years in [skill 1], [skill 2] and [skill 3]. [Degree, College] (optional).

Recently built [project name]: [one line + result]. Link: [GitHub / demo link]

If you're open to it, a referral would mean a lot. I can send my resume anytime.

Thanks a lot!
[Your Name]

3. Cold email to a founder

Use when: you can find the founder's email (company website, LinkedIn, Twitter / X).

Email

Subject: [Your role] who can help with [their product area]

Hi [Name],

I've been following [Company] and what you're building with [product]. [One honest line on why it caught your eye].

I'm a [your role] with [X] years of experience in [skill 1], [skill 2] and [skill 3].

One project close to what you're building:
[Project name]: [what it does], [result or number]. Link: [GitHub / demo link]

An idea for [Company]: [one line on how you would improve or build something for them].

Open to a 15 minute call this week? My resume is attached.

Best,
[Your Name]
[LinkedIn link] | [Portfolio / GitHub link]

4. Follow-up (after 3-4 days, only once)

Follow-up

Hi [Name], just following up on my last message.

I'd love to help with [specific thing]. Here's my [project name] in case it's useful: [link].

Thanks!

5. How to crack the founder interview

  • Before the call, use their product. Read their website, posts and job description.
  • Guess the problem they are stuck on right now. Think how you would solve it.
  • In the call they will talk about their current project. No DSA, no system design.
  • Give a relevant solution and a clear path: "We can solve this in these 3 steps."
  • Talk about your own project the same way: problem, what you built, result.
  • If they are convinced, the next round is with the founder. Then the offer.

Quick checklist before you hit send

  • Name and company spelled right
  • First line is personal, not copy-paste
  • One project with a working link
  • One clear ask (chat, referral or call)
  • Under 80 words
  • No grammar mistakes (read it once out loud)

Where to find founders

  • LinkedIn search: "Founder" or "Co-Founder" + "AI" + your city
  • Funding news posts on LinkedIn (startups that just raised are hiring)
  • Startup job boards and founder posts saying "we're hiring"
